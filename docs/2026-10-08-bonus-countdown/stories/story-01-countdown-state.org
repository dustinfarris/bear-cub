From §Key Behaviors / ~BearCub.Countdown~ — pure state (verbatim):

#+begin_quote
A new pure module: no clock, no config, no database. ~local_now~, the lead time and the seconds switch arrive as arguments.

#+begin_src elixir
@spec state(DateTime.t() | nil, boolean(), DateTime.t(), pos_integer(), pos_integer()) ::
        nil | %{mode: :minutes | :seconds, remaining_ms: pos_integer(), fraction: float()}
def state(cutoff, eligible?, local_now, lead_ms, switch_ms)

@spec next_edge(DateTime.t() | nil, DateTime.t(), pos_integer(), pos_integer()) :: DateTime.t() | nil
def next_edge(cutoff, local_now, lead_ms, switch_ms)
#+end_src

- ~state/5~ is ~nil~ when not eligible, when ~cutoff~ is ~nil~, when ~remaining <= 0~, or when ~remaining > lead~. Otherwise ~:seconds~ when ~remaining <= switch~, else ~:minutes~.
- ~fraction~ drives the dial: ~remaining / lead~ in minutes mode, ~remaining / switch~ in seconds mode.
- Displayed value: in minutes mode ~div(remaining_s, 60)~ where ~remaining_s = div(remaining_ms, 1000)~ — from the lead down to the switch, never =0 min=; in seconds mode ~m:ss~ of ~ceil(remaining_ms / 1000)~ — =5:00= down to =0:01=, then hidden. A tap during =0:01= still earns, matching the strict verdict ([[file:../decisions.org::#d133][D133]]).
- ~next_edge/4~ is the next instant the display changes, or ~nil~ once the cutoff has passed: appearance (~cutoff − lead~), the next whole minute in minutes mode (~cutoff − k·60 s~), the next whole second in seconds mode (~cutoff − k·1 s~), and the cutoff itself. Every edge is measured back from the cutoff, so ticks never drift ([[file:../decisions.org::#d145][D145]]).
#+end_quote

* User Story

As a kid racing a bonus cutoff, I need the time I have left to be worked out exactly from my own cutoff and the real time, so that the countdown I will see counts whole minutes and then seconds to the instant the bonus stops paying, and is gone from that instant on.

* Acceptance Criteria

** [ ] With no cutoff, with the bonus not earnable, with more than the lead time left, exactly at the cutoff, and after it, there is no countdown. (SC-1, SC-3, SC-4)
** [ ] From the lead time down to just above the seconds switch the countdown is in minutes; at or below the switch, down to the last millisecond before the cutoff, it is in seconds. (SC-2)
** [ ] In minutes the shown value runs from the lead (=30=) down to the switch (=5=) and is never =0 min=; in seconds it runs from =5:00= down to =0:01= and is never =0:00=. (SC-2, SC-4)
** [ ] The dial's fill drains from full to empty over the lead time in minutes, and refills to full at the switch and drains again over the seconds stretch. (SC-2)
** [ ] The next moment the display changes is known ahead of time — the countdown's appearance, each whole minute, the switch, each whole second, and the cutoff, each measured back from the cutoff — and there is none once the cutoff has passed. (SC-2, SC-4)

- The Design excerpt above is the source of truth for this story; [[file:../DESIGN.org][DESIGN.org]] is broader context only.

- New module ~lib/bear_cub/countdown.ex~, tests in ~test/bear_cub/countdown_test.exs~. Every test passes an explicit ~local_now~ and cutoff; no clock reads anywhere (CLAUDE.md "All time logic takes a local datetime argument").
- The Testing list puts the displayed-value cases in ~countdown_test.exs~, so the minutes / ~m:ss~ formatting belongs in ~BearCub.Countdown~ (a public function the component in Story 05 calls), not in the component. The design does not name the function; pick one and record its name in this story's Plan note for Story 05.
- Config validation (~validate_config!/1~ or equivalent) is in the same module but is Story 02's scope.
- Boundary arithmetic: ~remaining~ is ~DateTime.diff(cutoff, local_now, :millisecond)~. Minutes edges are ~cutoff − k·60 s~ and second edges ~cutoff − k·1 s~, so the minutes display (~div(remaining_s, 60)~) and the seconds display (~ceil~ of seconds) change exactly on those edges; test the edge just before and just after the switch to confirm ~next_edge/4~ never skips the switch.
