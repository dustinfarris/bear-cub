* Design

Verbatim from DESIGN.org for this story's scope; org links are repointed one level up so they resolve from ~stories/~.

** Admin — ~Admin.TodayLive~

A standing row directly beneath each kid's colored card header, above the routine sections. Phone-first and ~max-w-md~, so it stays compact.

- *In standing*: three small ~hero-star-solid~ in success green with the text *In good standing*. Text is right here — admin's reader is a parent on a phone, not a pre-reader across a kitchen, so the kiosk's no-text rule does not carry over.
- *Not in standing*: a muted *Not in good standing* verdict, followed by two half-chips.

The half-chips are *Last night* and *This morning*, each showing ✓ or ✗. A chip shows ✗ whenever its half is unsatisfied *or* failed; the two causes are not distinguished on this surface.

*** Why the chips are not redundant

Admin already lists today's morning section with its ~done/total~, so "this morning" is partly visible. *Last night's evening routine is nowhere in the app today, on any surface.* That half is the genuinely new information and the one a parent cannot check any other way.

*** The copy hazard

Today's standing reads *last* night's evening routine; *tonight's* evening routine feeds *tomorrow's* standing. A parent glancing at the card at 20:00 while the evening routine is half-done can easily read the standing row as commenting on the routine in front of them. The chip label must be *Last night*, never *Evening*. This is a design constraint, not implementation taste.

*** When it shows

Whenever ~Admin.TodayLive~ renders, *independent of the routine window* — unlike the kiosk's morning-window-only lifetime. The parent checking at 19:00 whether today was a standing day is the case this exists for, and by then the verdict is settled.

** Testing (excerpt)

LiveView integration tests are acceptance criteria, not optional extras.

*LiveView, admin* (~admin/today_live_test.exs~): the in-standing verdict; the not-in-standing verdict with the correct half-chips; the *Last night* label present.

*Test hygiene*: every test passes fixed local dates and pins ~:routine_windows~. No new test derives its date from the wall clock.

* User Story

As a parent on my phone, I need to see at any hour whether each kid is in good standing today and — when they are not — which of the two routines broke it, so that I can settle the screen-time question without arguing from memory, and so I can finally see whether last night's evening routine was actually finished.

* Technical Notes

The Design excerpt above is the source of truth for this story's spec; DESIGN.org is broader context only.

- This story depends on Story 02, which supplies ~Chores.standing/2~ and its per-half booleans.
- It is sequenced before the kiosk story deliberately: this is the surface that says the verdict in words, so it makes standing observable while the kiosk's wordless treatment is being built and QA'd on-device.
- ~Admin.TodayLive~'s ~load/2~ already receives a local datetime at every entry point and derives ~today~ from it; the standing call takes that same date. No clock read is added.
- A chip's ✗ condition is "not satisfied or failed" — with ~standing/2~ returning raw ~empty?~/~complete?~/~failed?~ per half, the render resolves it; the derivation is not changed to pre-resolve an atom.
- This surface is advisory only: nothing on this view gates or is gated on standing.
- The new ~today_live_test.exs~ tests capture ~LocalTime.now()~ once in the describe's ~setup~ and reuse that single binding (~now~/~yesterday~) everywhere, rather than re-deriving from the wall clock a second time within a test — but they do not call ~pin_active_routine/1~. This is deliberate, not an oversight: ~Chores.standing/2~ and ~routine_day_status/3~ never read ~:routine_windows~ at all (standing is pure ~Date~ arithmetic, independent of which routine window is open), so pinning it here would guard against a failure mode that cannot occur for this surface. The Design excerpt's Test hygiene line ("pins ~:routine_windows~") describes the kiosk/window-dependent tests elsewhere in this suite; it does not fit a window-independent surface literally.
