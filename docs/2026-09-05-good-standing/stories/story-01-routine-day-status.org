** ~Chores.routine_day_status/3~ — the shared per-routine-day predicate

"Every roster chore has a live completion, and any completion carries ~failed_at~" is currently written twice: in ~routine_day_contribution/3~ (per-kid, two queries) and in ~routine_days_by_kid/1~ (the grouped-SQL aggregate). Standing would make it three. Extract the per-~(kid, routine, date)~ half out of ~routine_day_contribution/3~:

#+begin_src elixir
Chores.routine_day_status(kid, "evening", ~D[2026-09-04])
#=> %{empty?: false, complete?: true, failed?: false}
#+end_src

~routine_day_contribution/3~ becomes a thin points wrapper over it, and ~standing/2~ calls it twice. The implementation count stays at two rather than rising to three.

Deliberately *not* touched: ~routine_days_by_kid/1~. Folding the grouped SQL into the same abstraction is a larger change than this item earns.

** ~complete?~ is vacuously true for an empty roster

~complete?~ means "every chore on this roster has a live completion", which holds trivially when there are none. ~empty?~ is carried *separately* rather than folded into it, so each consumer applies its own reading:

| Consumer                     | Condition                  | Empty roster yields |
|------------------------------+----------------------------+---------------------|
| ~standing/2~                 | ~complete?~                | satisfied           |
| ~routine_day_contribution/3~ | ~complete? and not empty?~ | no ~+R~ bonus       |

This is an inversion of the pre-existing points reading, and it is the pivot the whole empty-roster ruling turns on. For points, a kid with no chores must not collect ~+R~ for free. For standing, nothing was asked, so nothing was missed. Both readings live in their consumers rather than in the shared status, and neither has to re-derive the other's rule.

One rule settles three cases:

| Case                                            | Result                       |
|-------------------------------------------------+------------------------------|
| Kid has zero evening chores configured          | evening half satisfied       |
| Kid added yesterday or today (no history)       | evening half satisfied       |
| Roster exists, nothing tapped (away, sleepover) | evening half *not* satisfied |

The consequence for the *morning* half, which takes the same reading for consistency: a kid with zero morning chores is vacuously satisfied all day, so their band appears at window open rather than at a completion. Degenerate but coherent, and the kiosk timing below handles it explicitly.

** Testing (excerpt)

*Refactor safety*: the existing points tests must pass untouched. That is the whole evidence that extracting ~routine_day_status/3~ was behavior-preserving. Add one test the existing suite may not carry explicitly — *an empty roster earns no ~+R~* — since the empty-roster condition moves from inside ~complete?~ out to the points consumer, and nothing else would catch it regressing.

*Test hygiene*: every test passes fixed local dates and pins ~:routine_windows~. No new test derives its date from the wall clock.

* User Story

As a developer of Bear Cub, I need the "was this routine-day finished, and was it failed?" rule to exist in one place that both points and standing read — so that the one meaning of a finished routine cannot drift into two, and so that adding standing does not add a third copy of a rule the existing two have already disagreed over once.

* Technical Notes

The Design excerpt above is the source of truth for this story's spec; DESIGN.org is broader context only.

- This story has no dependencies on earlier stories in this batch. Stories 02–05 all depend on it: ~standing/2~ calls ~routine_day_status/3~ twice.
- The roster query being extracted already carries the chore-lifetime bound from the chore-lifecycle initiative (~active_from <= date~ and ~archived_on~ nil-or-later). That bound must survive the extraction verbatim — it is what makes standing's evening half score against the chores that were live *last night* for free. The existing points tests, which cover the archive-day case, are the guard; Story 02 adds the roster-history guard at the standing level.
- The three-case table in the excerpt describes standing-level consequences of this story's ~empty?~/~complete?~ split; those cases are Story 02's acceptance criteria, not this story's.
- ~routine_day_contribution/3~ keeps its arity and its meaning — it becomes a wrapper computing ~+R~ on ~complete? and not empty?~ and ~−R~ on ~failed?~.
