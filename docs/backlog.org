~Rewards.spend_totals_by_kid/1~ filters on a redemption row's ~local_date~ (request time) combined with its *current* ~approved_at~/~reversed_at~ state, not the state as of the date being queried, so a marker stamped after the fact changes what an earlier "as of" read reports — the same D10/D72-class characteristic as the roster-drift entry above, not a bug. D73's day-scoped ~check_open/2~ closes the original multi-day form (request day 1, an intermediate ~Points.balance~ read on day 2, approval on day 3 moving that day-2 read after the fact) — ~approve_redemption/3~ now refuses a request once its ~local_date~ is before today. What remains, still accepted under D72's reasoning: (a) the *same-day* case — a request and its approval both dated today, with an intermediate ~Points.balance~ read taken between the two, still moves after the fact, since ~check_open/2~ only excludes prior days, not same-day timing; (b) direct data manipulation (IEx, or any future admin action stamping ~approved_at~ outside the guarded write paths) can still produce a lapsed-then-approved row, bypassing the guard entirely.

:PROPERTIES:

Considered during the routine-schedule design and kept out of that initiative ([[file:decisions.org::#d127][D127]]). Commanded's only officially supported production event store is the PostgreSQL-backed EventStore (the in-memory adapter is test-only), so adopting it means moving the home server off SQLite, rewriting each context around aggregates and projections, and storing points in projections rather than deriving them on read (D10). The app already holds two append-only fact logs — ~completions~ and ~schedule_versions~ — which give derive-on-read the history it needs. Trigger: a concrete want for an audit trail of every parent and kid action, replaying history under new rules, or read models that derive-on-read cannot serve; make that case as its own PRD, not as a side effect of another feature.
