#+title: Story 05 — Kiosk shop: the ~:rewards~ column state, banner button, and card precedence
#+date: 2026-07-25

Design: [[file:../DESIGN.org]]

* Design

Verbatim from DESIGN.org for this story's scope.

** Kiosk — the reward view

A new per-column state ~:rewards~, beside the shipped ~:rows~ and ~:band~. The column *body* — the events strip, the routine card region, and extras — swaps to the reward catalog; the kid-color banner stays, so the points badge is visible while shopping and the column remains identifiable by color at a distance. The sibling's column is untouched and fully usable.

Because ~:rewards~ replaces the routine region rather than sitting beside it, *the 5-chore no-scroll budget is untouched*. The only permanent addition to the column is the banner button, which is horizontal.

** Kiosk — the banner button

The banner becomes ~[completion icon] NAME [★ points] [🎁]~ — the gift button grouped with the points badge on the right, because the badge and the shop are the same economy and read as one unit.

The button has exactly *two* states: =🎁= idle, and =⏳= while this kid has a pending request today. A verdict returns it to =🎁=.

Decline visibility is *in-shop only*: the declined glyph rides the card (precedence row 4) and is there whenever the child next looks, while the banner returns to =🎁=. An approval needs no banner state either — it drops the always-visible points badge by the reward's price, the most legible ambient signal the kiosk has.

The points badge's own tap gesture stays *unbound* — the gift button is a distinct gesture, so the deferred points-stats affordance is not precluded.

** Kiosk — card states, one precedence table

Verdict states and availability states are the same kind of thing — derived from redemption markers plus ~local_date == today~ — so they resolve in one ordered table rather than two overlapping systems. Evaluated top-down, first match wins:

| # | condition                                                        | rendering               | tappable |
|---+------------------------------------------------------------------+-------------------------+----------|
| 1 | ~retired_at~ set                                                 | absent                  | —        |
| 2 | one-time, this kid consumed it, and that redemption is not today | absent                  | —        |
| 3 | this kid has a pending request for this reward today             | =⏳= pending treatment  | no       |
| 4 | this kid was declined for this reward today                      | dim + declined glyph    | no       |
| 5 | this kid claimed this reward today                               | dim + check glyph       | no       |
| 6 | ~Points.balance(kid, today) < reward.points~                     | dim + lock glyph        | no       |
| 7 | otherwise                                                        | full-color card         | *yes*    |

Notes on the ordering and the choices:

- *Claimed (5) outranks unaffordable (6)* — after a spend the balance is usually below the price again, and "you got this today" is strictly more informative than "you can't afford this".
- *Rows 4–6 share one dim treatment and differ only by glyph* — declined, check (claimed today), lock (can't afford). A pre-reader learns three glyphs against *one* layout rather than three layouts, and the pending row's =⏳= is the same glyph already carried by the banner button. Extending the existing "not now" vocabulary costs no new visual grammar (SC-6).
- *Consumed one-time rewards show as claimed for the rest of the local day, then disappear.* A child who saved 500 for the ATM toy should not watch it vanish the instant it is granted — that reads as a bug rather than a triumph. Showing "claimed" permanently is the other failure: dead cards would accumulate forever in a catalog a pre-reader scans by glance.
- *Repeatable rewards on cooldown are dimmed, not hidden* — a catalog that changes shape day to day with no visible reason is the least explicable thing a shelf can do.
- *Row 4 doubles as the re-ask block.* A declined row does not occupy the one-open-request index slot (it has ~declined_at~ set), so without this rendering rule a child could re-tap the same reward immediately and re-queue it indefinitely. Deriving "declined for this reward today" and rendering it untappable closes the loop with no new storage — the D55 day-scope pattern.

** Kiosk — night (D56)

D56 outranks ~:rewards~ *absolutely*. Between 23:00 and 05:00 the kiosk renders one dark screen with one moon and nothing else — no columns, therefore no banner, no gift button, no reward view, and nothing rewards-related that could be tapped. Because columns simply do not render at night, this needs no special case: the state is unreachable rather than suppressed. An idle timer that happens to fire at night is a harmless no-op re-render.

** Kiosk — design-language recordings

The reward card surface, the dim+glyph vocabulary for the three unavailable reasons, and the =🎁= / =⏳= banner button are all *new kids'-view indicators*. Per the D48 / D54 pattern they are *pending design-language recordings* — written into ~docs/design-language.org~ by the story that lands them, not at design time. The existing "do not add further indicators without a ruling" clause on the completion badge makes this mandatory, not optional.

** Kiosk interaction

One tap to ask — *no modal, no confirmation*, with ~phx-throttle~ matching the shipped chore row. This is the deliberate contrast to D53: D53 guards actions that are *irreversible* and punitive, and a request is neither — the parent gate *is* the protection, and a mistaken ask costs a decline. A confirmation dialog is also the wrong instrument on this surface, since a pre-reader cannot read it.

No kid-side withdraw. The escapes are the parent's decline and the overnight lapse.

Dismiss and idle auto-return: an explicit ✕ closes the view. In addition, an idle timer returns the column to its normal state if the child wanders off — a ~Process.send_after~ armed on open, reset on each tap inside the view, and cancelled on dismiss. The duration is a module constant tuned at the on-device gate, exactly as ~@collapse_delay_ms~ (currently 400ms) is; this design deliberately does not pin a number, though ~30s is the starting guess.

After a successful request the view returns to the normal column immediately and the banner button flips to =⏳=, so the pending state lives where the child can see it without standing in the shop.

State is *ephemeral*: ~:rewards~ membership is a socket-level ~MapSet~ of kid ids, exactly like the shipped ~@expanded~ — never persisted, cleared by the timer, and cleared on the routine boundary re-render.

** From hidden debt

- *Kiosk keeps the floor* (D41, D43). The *lock glyph on an unaffordable card is the pre-reader-legible explanation* — a child who cannot read a negative number can read a lock. No number is needed for the child to act correctly (SC-3, SC-6).

* User Story

As a kid at the fridge, I need to open a shop from my own banner, see at a glance which rewards I can have right now and which I cannot, and ask for one with a single tap — so that I can spend what I have saved without reading a word or waiting for anyone to hand me the tablet.

* Technical Notes

The Design excerpt above is the source of truth; DESIGN.org is broader context.

- Depends on Story 03 (the request write path, the availability and affordability derivations, and the ="rewards"= topic) and on Story 04 (a real catalog to render, so this view can be exercised against data a parent actually created). Balances come from ~Points~ (Stories 01–02).
- *Read ~docs/design-language.org~ before touching styling* — its invariants and rulings are binding, and this story adds three Rulings to it. The recordings are ACs, not follow-up work.
- ~:rewards~ joins the shipped per-column state machine beside ~:rows~ and ~:band~; the ~MapSet~ assign parallels the shipped ~@expanded~ / ~@pending_collapse~ pattern in ~lib/bear_cub_web/live/kiosk_live.ex~.
- The idle timer follows the shipped ~@collapse_delay_ms~ precedent: a module constant, ~Process.send_after~, and a ~handle_info/2~ clause. The design deliberately does not pin the duration (~30s is the starting guess) — it is tuned at the on-device gate that closes this batch.
- ~KioskLive~ subscribes to ="rewards"= in addition to ="chores"= — one extra ~handle_info/2~ clause, re-fetching rather than patching from a payload (D71).
- The card precedence table is one ordered resolution, not two systems: implement it as a single first-match function returning the card's state atom, so the seven integration tests each pin one row of the table.
- Stable DOM ids for the tests: ~#gift-button-{kid.id}~, ~#reward-card-{reward.id}-{kid.id}~ (or equivalent), and a dismiss control id. Test against elements, never raw HTML.
- The 5-chore no-scroll budget is untouched by construction — the view replaces the routine region. The banner button is the only permanent addition, and confirming the budget still holds with it present is part of the on-device gate at batch close.
