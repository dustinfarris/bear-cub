* Overview

Points stop being a number that only goes up. A parent maintains a catalog of rewards in admin — name, emoji, price, and whether it is offered to one child or both (SC-1). On the kiosk a child opens a reward view from their own banner, sees at a glance what their points can buy, and taps to /ask/ for one; a parent approves or declines from their phone, and may also redeem directly with no request at all, for when the family is away from the tablet (SC-2). An approved redemption spends the points permanently (SC-3).

This is the first /non-completion signed input/ to the points total. The gamification initiative shaped the total as ~max(0, completions_earnings + other_signed_inputs)~ with the second term empty and named redemption as the anticipated occupant (D42); this initiative makes that term literal and does not re-derive earnings at all. It also exercises the pre-authorized raw-balance seam: the zero floor is display-only and applies to the aggregate, so a balance-needing feature sums the raw signed contributions directly (D41). Affordability is checked against that /raw signed balance/, never the floored display figure, so every penalty a child has taken counts against what they can have (SC-4).

Two new tables carry it — ~rewards~ and ~redemptions~ — and nothing about ~chores~ or ~completions~ changes. Redemption state is *derived from nullable timestamp markers*, never a status column, exactly as ~undone_at~ and ~failed_at~ already are: the row records what happened and when, and pending / lapsed / approved / reversed / declined are all queries over those facts (honoring D10). Availability is derived the same way: a one-time reward is consumed for a child once they hold an unreversed approval for it, and a repeatable reward is on cooldown for the rest of the local day — so "available again tomorrow" arrives by the date rolling over, with no job and no midnight machinery. Both predicates are scoped *per kid*; ~rewards.kid_id~ is an administrative audience control and carries no scarcity meaning (SC-5).

Redemption also makes the points total load-bearing: it is read on every reward-card render, on every tap that checks affordability, and on every admin balance display. The shipped ~points_total/2~ issues on the order of one query per routine-day of history, which is why PRD SC-9 requires the check be cheap and /stay/ cheap as months accumulate. The deferred aggregate rewrite is therefore claimed here as a batch member and lands early, before affordability depends on it — as a pure performance refactor with semantics provably unchanged.

Everything the kid sees stays pre-reader legible: a locked card, a claimed card, and a declined card share one dim treatment and differ only by glyph and glyph color, so a child learns three signs against one layout rather than three layouts (SC-6). The kiosk keeps the zero floor and never shows a negative number; the /admin/ surfaces show the true signed balance, negative and all, because the parent is the only party who can act on debt.

** Context this design builds on (restated, self-contained)

Facts from shipped work that this initiative depends on and does not reshape:

- *The redemption seam* (D42) — the total is ~max(0, completions_earnings + other_signed_inputs)~, with ~other_signed_inputs~ empty today. A spend is a negative, non-completion signed input slotting into that term. Earnings derivation is untouched.
- *The zero floor is display-only, on the aggregate* (D41) — signed rows are never clamped; the raw signed sum is the true balance, and D41 states outright that a future balance-needing feature sums the raw signed contributions directly.
- *The always-visible per-kid points badge* (D43) lives in the kid-color banner and shows the floored total, tied to child identity. D41 and D43 together are the authority for the kiosk never showing a negative number.
- *Derived state doctrine* (D10) — day state is a query over dated facts. No status flags, no reset jobs, no midnight machinery.
- *Every scoring event so far is a completion event* (D39), with redemptions named as the anticipated first non-completion input; new storage is expected here, and its shape is argued in Data Model rather than forced onto ~completions~.
- *Audit permanence* — signed records persist forever and are never erased. A reversal is a marker, never a deletion.
- *Irreversible, point-affecting admin actions are confirmation-guarded* (D53); the shipped precedent is ~data-confirm~ on the fail control in ~lib/bear_cub_web/live/admin/today_live.ex~.
- *The day-scoped standing-evidence pattern* (D55) — admin state derived from "did this happen today", independent of current object state, resetting by date rollover rather than by a job.
- *The night screen* ([[file:../decisions.org::#d56][D56]]) — between 23:00 and 05:00 the kiosk is one dark screen with one evening-colored moon and nothing else; no columns render and nothing is interactive.
- *Nullable discriminator over a parallel table* (D27; ~docs/learnings.org~ [2026-07-12]) — give a new entity an existing entity's machinery via a nullable column. Confirmed pattern, applied twice already.
- *Payload-free PubSub* — writes broadcast a bare message; subscribers re-fetch and never patch from a payload.
- *Kiosk/admin fence* — the kiosk contains zero links or navigation to ~/admin~.
- *Contexts do not reach sideways* — ~BearCub.Chores~ must never depend on ~BearCub.Calendars~; the same discipline governs the new context.
- *Pre-reader glance legibility* — a child who cannot read must be able to act correctly on glance. Kid names never enter git.

* Data Model

Two new tables. ~completions~ and ~chores~ are untouched.

** ~rewards~

| column       | type                 | notes                                                                              |
|--------------+----------------------+------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| ~kid_id~     | FK, *nullable*       | NULL = offered to any kid; set = offered to that kid only. Audience, never scarcity |
| ~name~       | string, NOT NULL     |                                                                                    |
| ~icon~       | string, NOT NULL     | emoji, per the chore-icon convention; form default =🎁=                             |
| ~points~     | integer, NOT NULL    | the price                                                                          |
| ~repeatable~ | boolean, NOT NULL    | default ~true~                                                                     |
| ~position~   | integer, NOT NULL    | parent-controlled order                                                            |
| ~retired_at~ | ~utc_datetime~, NULL | soft archive                                                                       |

~kid_id~ is set programmatically from the form's kid selection and never cast from attrs, matching the shipped ~create_chore/2~ treatment.

Why *nullable ~kid_id~* rather than a family-only or per-kid-only catalog: the nullable discriminator is the repo's confirmed pattern (D27; ~docs/learnings.org~ [2026-07-12]). One column buys both models, and the two knobs stay orthogonal — "who is this offered to" (~kid_id~) is a separate question from "how often may a kid claim it" (~repeatable~). ~list_rewards(kid, local_date)~ is ~where is_nil(kid_id) or kid_id == ^kid.id~, not retired, ordered by ~position~.

~kid_id~ is *purely an administrative audience control*: when a parent creates a reward they are answering "is this on offer to both kids, or just one?" — nothing more. It says nothing about how many times the reward can be claimed; that is ~repeatable~'s job, and both questions are answered per-kid (Key Behaviors → availability).

A one-time reward offered to *both* kids ("your own bike" — each of them may earn one) and a repeatable kid-scoped reward ("an extra bedtime story for Ollie") are both legal and both meaningful; the two knobs are never coupled.

~repeatable~ is *a boolean, not a cooldown interval*. Repeatable means available again the next local day — a fixed one-day cooldown. A ~cooldown_days~ integer would be strictly more general and would collapse both concepts into one field, but nothing in family use asks for a 3-day or 7-day cooldown; if it ever arrives, ~repeatable~ widens to ~cooldown_days~ additively.

** ~redemptions~ — the D42 signed input

| column         | type                 | notes                                                 |
|----------------+----------------------+---------------------------------------------------------|
| ~kid_id~       | FK, NOT NULL         | set programmatically, never cast                      |
| ~reward_id~    | FK, NOT NULL         |                                                       |
| ~points~       | integer, NOT NULL    | *price snapshot* taken at row creation                |
| ~local_date~   | date, NOT NULL       | the local day the row was created                     |
| ~requested_at~ | ~utc_datetime~, NULL | NULL = parent direct-redeem (no kid request)          |
| ~approved_at~  | ~utc_datetime~, NULL |                                                       |
| ~declined_at~  | ~utc_datetime~, NULL |                                                       |
| ~withdrawn_at~ | ~utc_datetime~, NULL | the kid-side withdraw marker (D76)                    |
| ~reversed_at~  | ~utc_datetime~, NULL | the reversal marker                                   |
| ~source~       | string, NOT NULL     | ="kiosk"= / ="admin"=, mirroring ~completions.source~  |

Contribution is marker-first, mirroring the shipped ~extra_contribution/2~ ~cond~ exactly:

#+begin_src
reversed_at set  ->  0        (reversed; row retained forever as the trail)
approved_at set  -> −points   (the spend)
otherwise        ->  0        (pending, declined, withdrawn, or lapsed)
#+end_src

State is derived from nullable timestamp markers, *never a status column* — D10's posture and the ~undone_at~ / ~failed_at~ precedent. ~withdrawn_at~ is the fourth marker and takes the same shape as the other three: a withdrawn row is retained forever, contributes 0, and is invisible to ~pending?~ (D76):

#+begin_src
open       = requested_at && !approved_at && !declined_at && !withdrawn_at
pending?   = open && local_date == today
lapsed?    = open && local_date <  today
approved?  = approved_at  && !reversed_at
reversed?  = approved_at  &&  reversed_at
declined?  = declined_at
withdrawn? = withdrawn_at
#+end_src

The write paths guard on ~open~ plus ~local_date == today~ in both directions: ~withdraw~ refuses a row already approved or declined, and ~approve~ / ~decline~ refuse a row already withdrawn, all returning the ~{:error, :not_open}~ family — the same guard shape lapse already gets (D73).

The price snapshot is *non-negotiable*. The total is cumulative all-time and purely derived, so reading a live ~rewards.points~ at derivation time would make history mutable: a parent raising a reward's price would retroactively deepen every past spend of it and silently move a child's balance, potentially into debt after the fact. The snapshot is taken at *request* time for the kid leg — that is the deal the child was shown — and at redeem time for the parent-direct leg (SC-7).

A ~reward_name~ snapshot is deliberately *not* taken: reward rows are kept forever (archive, never delete), so the join is always available and the name stays a single source of truth. A rename is correctly reflected in history — a parent renaming a reward is clarifying what it is, not creating a different one.

** Indexes

#+begin_src sql
-- one open request per kid per reward per local day (D77)
CREATE UNIQUE INDEX redemptions_one_open_per_kid_reward
  ON redemptions (kid_id, reward_id, local_date)
  WHERE requested_at IS NOT NULL AND approved_at IS NULL AND declined_at IS NULL
    AND withdrawn_at IS NULL;
#+end_src

The same partial-unique-index technique the shipped ~completions (chore_id, local_date) WHERE undone_at IS NULL~ index already uses. Including ~local_date~ in the key is what makes lapse *free*: yesterday's unanswered request cannot block today's, with no job and no cleanup (SC-6).

The key carries a ~reward_id~ component, so what it constrains is *duplicate open asks for the same reward*, never how many rewards a kid may have open at once (D77) — a kid may hold several pending requests, bounded by the rendering reserve rule in Key Behaviors → affordability rather than by this index. Same-reward duplicates are already impossible through the UI, since a pending card's tap is a withdraw (D76); the index is there to catch a race, exactly the posture the one-open-request index took before it.

A racing duplicate hits the index and returns ~{:error, changeset}~, which callers treat as already-asked — the same posture ~complete_chore/3~ takes on a racing double-tap.

Because SQLite cannot alter an index in place, the migration *drops the old index and creates the new one*; it is a replacement, not an added constraint.

Supporting indexes on ~redemptions (reward_id)~ and ~(kid_id, local_date)~ serve the availability and balance reads.

* Screens / Interfaces

** Kiosk — the reward view

A new per-column state ~:rewards~, beside the shipped ~:rows~ and ~:band~. The column *body* — the events strip, the routine card region, and extras — swaps to the reward catalog; the kid-color banner stays, so the points badge is visible while shopping and the column remains identifiable by color at a distance. The sibling's column is untouched and fully usable.

Because ~:rewards~ replaces the routine region rather than sitting beside it, *the 5-chore no-scroll budget is untouched*. The only permanent addition to the column is the banner button, which is horizontal.

** Kiosk — the banner button

The banner becomes ~[completion icon] NAME [★ points] [🎁]~ — the gift button grouped with the points badge on the right, because the badge and the shop are the same economy and read as one unit.

The button has exactly *two* states: =🎁= idle, and =⏳= while this kid has *any* open request today. It returns to =🎁= once none remains — by verdict, by withdraw (D76), or by lapse; with several asks open at once (D77) a single verdict does not necessarily clear it.

The button is also the shop's *toggle*: tapping it while the shop is already open closes the column back to normal, whichever glyph it is showing (D75). It is always tappable, and its glyph reports the request state rather than gating the tap.

Decline visibility is *in-shop only*: the declined glyph rides the card (precedence row 4) and is there whenever the child next looks, while the banner returns to =🎁=. An approval needs no banner state either — it drops the always-visible points badge by the reward's price, the most legible ambient signal the kiosk has.

The points badge's own tap gesture stays *unbound* — the gift button is a distinct gesture, so the deferred points-stats affordance is not precluded.

** Kiosk — card states, one precedence table

Verdict states and availability states are the same kind of thing — derived from redemption markers plus ~local_date == today~ — so they resolve in one ordered table rather than two overlapping systems. Evaluated top-down, first match wins:

| # | condition                                                        | rendering               | tappable          |
|---+------------------------------------------------------------------+-------------------------+-------------------|
| 1 | ~retired_at~ set                                                 | absent                  | —                 |
| 2 | one-time, this kid consumed it, and that redemption is not today | absent                  | —                 |
| 3 | this kid has a pending request for this reward today             | =⏳= pending treatment  | *yes* — withdraws |
| 4 | this kid was declined for this reward today                      | dim + red ✕ glyph       | no                |
| 5 | this kid claimed this reward today                               | dim + green check glyph | no                |
| 6 | ~balance − pending_total(kid, today) < reward.points~            | dim + lock glyph        | no                |
| 7 | otherwise                                                        | full-color card         | *yes* — asks      |

Notes on the ordering and the choices:

- *Row 3 is the withdraw tap* (D76). A pending card is full-color and tappable, and its tap takes the request back rather than re-asking — no confirmation, since asking again is the undo. The card falls straight back to its plain state (row 7, or row 6 if the withdrawn commitment was what a sibling card's lock depended on).
- *Claimed (5) outranks unaffordable (6)* — after a spend the balance is usually below the price again, and "you got this today" is strictly more informative than "you can't afford this".
- *Rows 4–6 share one dim treatment and differ only by glyph and glyph color* — a red ✕ (declined), a green check (claimed today), a lock (can't afford). A pre-reader learns three signs against *one* layout rather than three layouts, and the pending row's =⏳= is the same glyph already carried by the banner button. Extending the existing "not now" vocabulary costs no new visual grammar (SC-6). The colors are load-bearing rather than decorative: an uncolored ✕ reads as a close control on a surface that has one, and the claimed check earns the same success green a done chore row already carries.
- *Consumed one-time rewards show as claimed for the rest of the local day, then disappear.* A child who saved 500 for the ATM toy should not watch it vanish the instant it is granted — that reads as a bug rather than a triumph. Showing "claimed" permanently is the other failure: dead cards would accumulate forever in a catalog a pre-reader scans by glance.
- *Repeatable rewards on cooldown are dimmed, not hidden* — a catalog that changes shape day to day with no visible reason is the least explicable thing a shelf can do.
- *Row 4 doubles as the re-ask block.* A declined row does not occupy the open-ask index slot for its reward (it has ~declined_at~ set), so without this rendering rule a child could re-tap the same reward immediately and re-queue it indefinitely. Deriving "declined for this reward today" and rendering it untappable closes the loop with no new storage — the D55 day-scope pattern. A *withdrawn* row is deliberately not blocked the same way: withdrawing is the child's own correction, and re-asking is exactly what makes it costless (D76).
- *Row 6 is reserve-aware* (D77). The lock condition subtracts what this kid has already committed to in open requests, so a kid may hold several asks at once but never asks for more than their points cover. The subtraction is a rendering rule only: the ledger is untouched, a pending row still contributes 0, and the banner badge keeps showing the true floored total (Key Behaviors → affordability). A card whose own request is pending never self-locks, because row 3 resolves before row 6.

** Kiosk — night (D56)

D56 outranks ~:rewards~ *absolutely*. Between 23:00 and 05:00 the kiosk renders one dark screen with one moon and nothing else — no columns, therefore no banner, no gift button, no reward view, and nothing rewards-related that could be tapped. Because columns simply do not render at night, this needs no special case: the state is unreachable rather than suppressed. An idle timer that happens to fire at night is a harmless no-op re-render.

** Kiosk — design-language recordings

The reward card surface, the dim+glyph vocabulary for the three unavailable reasons, and the =🎁= / =⏳= banner button are all *new kids'-view indicators*. Per the D48 / D54 pattern they are *pending design-language recordings* — written into ~docs/design-language.org~ by the story that lands them, not at design time. The existing "do not add further indicators without a ruling" clause on the completion badge makes this mandatory, not optional.

** ~/admin/rewards~ — the catalog

A new route and a fifth bottom-nav entry (~:today, :chores, :kids, :calendars~ → plus ~:rewards~), scaffolded with ~mix phx.gen.live~ mirroring ~ChoreLive.Index~ / ~ChoreLive.Form~ per repo convention. The form carries name, emoji (default =🎁=), price, repeatable, and an optional kid ("Anyone" or a specific child) (SC-1).

Ordering is *~position~ with ▲/▼*. ~move_reward/2~ is written from the shipped ~move_chore/2~ as its template — the same neighbor-swap in a transaction, the same silent no-op at the list edge, the same broadcast. The ~is_nil/1~ bucketing discipline from ~docs/learnings.org~ [2026-07-12] does *not* apply here: the reward list is a single flat bucket, so the neighbor lookup has no nullable discriminator to bucket by.

Removal is *archive, never delete*: ~retired_at~ is the only removal affordance in this design. An archived reward vanishes from the kiosk catalog and from the direct-redeem picker, and remains in the history view with its redemptions intact (SC-7).

Direct-redeem lives here: a per-reward action opening a kid picker *scoped to the reward's audience* — showing the true signed balance of each kid the reward is offered to, never every kid regardless of ~kid_id~ — then a ~data-confirm~ guard, then the spend. Same affordability, availability, and audience rules as the kid leg — no override (SC-2, SC-4, D74).

The picker's per-kid figure is *labeled as that kid's balance* (=balance N ★=), never a bare number beside a name: on a row whose other number is a price, an unlabeled figure reads as a second price rather than as what the child has.

The action is worded *Redeem*, not "Give" — on the button, in its ~data-confirm~, and in the success flash. A direct-redeem spends the child's own points on their behalf; "give" describes a gift, which is the one thing this action is not, and the same wording carries to the approval control on ~Admin.TodayLive~.

** ~Admin.TodayLive~ — the approval queue and reversal

A per-kid *Requests* section alongside the existing Extras section, in today's stacked layout. Today is where a parent already looks, and a request is a day-shaped object — the same shape as everything else on that screen.

The kid's *true signed balance* rides the kid's own header band — ★ plus the signed figure, always visible, whether or not that kid has any requests today. It belongs to the child, not to any one ask: a kid may hold several open requests (D77), which would otherwise repeat the same number down the list, and a parent looking at a debt needs to see it whether or not a request happens to be waiting. A permanently visible negative number on this surface is correct and intended (D64).

Each pending request then shows the reward's icon and name and its snapshot price — the price only, no per-request balance line — with:

- *Approve* — ~data-confirm~ guarded (D53: points-affecting, and permanent from tomorrow onward, since the reversal control is day-scoped), worded *Redeem* like the direct-redeem control it mirrors. Re-validates affordability *and* availability before stamping ~approved_at~; a failed re-check tells the parent which check failed rather than silently no-opping. With several asks open, approving one can legitimately make another's re-check fail as unaffordable — the refusal display already covers it (D77).
- *Decline* — no confirmation. It costs nothing, contributes 0, and its own day-scoping is the undo.

Lapsed and withdrawn rows *never surface in the queue*. The queue reads open rows only — ~requested_at~ set, no verdict, not withdrawn, and ~local_date == today~. Yesterday's unanswered ask is invisible here by the same predicate that makes it invisible on the kiosk, with nothing to clean up (SC-6); a request the child takes back drops out of the queue live on the ~rewards~ broadcast, needing no parent action at all (D76).

Lapse is *terminal*, not merely display-hidden (D73): ~approve_redemption/3~ and ~decline_redemption/2~ refuse a lapsed row exactly as they refuse an already-answered one, both returning ~{:error, :not_open}~ — a lapsed request cannot be revived by a write path called directly, only by the queue simply never offering it.

Reverse lives on the same screen beside the day's redemptions, ~data-confirm~ guarded, and is *day-scoped* — offered only on redemptions with ~local_date == today~, following the D55 pattern (SC-8). Today is the correction surface, and a mis-tap gets caught the same day or not at all; a reversal reachable from arbitrary history would be an editing affordance over the permanent record rather than a same-day correction. A reversal's *effect* remains legible indefinitely in the history view, which is where the durable record belongs.

** ~/admin/rewards/history~ — the redemption record

A parent must be able to answer "did this child redeem this reward?" days later (SC-7). The storage already supports it — append-only rows, price snapshot, ~reversed_at~ marker — so this is purely a read surface.

It is *one flat reverse-chronological list*, its own route, linked from ~/admin/rewards~. Each row: date, kid (name + color chip), reward icon + name, snapshot price, and state (approved / reversed / declined). A reversed row renders its price struck through with a "reversed" marker and the reversal's date — this is where a reversal stays legible days later.

- At family scale — two kids, a handful of rewards, a few redemptions a week — one list beats per-kid or per-reward views: no navigation, no filter UI, and the whole record fits on a phone screen. Per-kid grouping can be added later if the list ever gets long enough to need it.
- *Shows approved, reversed, and declined; omits lapsed and withdrawn.* A never-answered ask is an abandoned intention, not history, and a withdrawn one is an intention the child took back (D76); including either would bury real redemptions in noise.
- *Archived rewards still appear* with their name and icon — the row is retained, and only the catalog and picker filter on ~retired_at~.
- *Bounded, visibly.* The list shows the 50 most recent entries with no pagination, and *says so on screen* — a silent truncation would read as "this is everything" when it is not. At the stated family volume 50 entries spans months, comfortably covering SC-7's "days afterward" horizon; pagination is a backlog seed if it ever bites.

** Balances in admin

Every admin surface that shows a balance shows the *true signed* figure, negative included (see Key Behaviors → hidden debt).

* Key Behaviors

** Contexts and dependency direction

Two new modules.

~BearCub.Rewards~ — a context owning the ~rewards~ and ~redemptions~ tables, the request / approve / decline / direct-redeem / reverse write paths, the availability derivation, and its own PubSub topic. It knows nothing about chores or completions.

~BearCub.Points~ — a composition module, the only place in the app that knows both earnings and spends:

#+begin_src elixir
Points.balance(kid, local_date)   # raw signed integer; may be negative
Points.total(kid, local_date)     # max(0, balance) — the D41 display figure
Points.balances(local_date)       # %{kid_id => signed} for a whole render
Points.totals(local_date)         # %{kid_id => floored} for a whole render
#+end_src

~Chores.points_total/2~ becomes ~Chores.earnings/2~ and returns the *raw signed* earnings, unfloored — the floor moves up to ~Points~, where the complete summation lives. ~Chores~ never learns that ~Rewards~ exists.

#+begin_src
Points.balance = Chores.earnings + Rewards.spend_total
                 └─ D42's completions_earnings   └─ D42's other_signed_inputs
#+end_src

Cost to shipped code, stated plainly: the kiosk's ~points: Chores.points_total(kid, today)~ call, admin balance reads, and the existing points-derivation tests all move to ~Points~. This is a rename plus a re-point, not a rewrite, and the aggregate rewrite touches the same function anyway.

** Availability and repeatability

Availability is *fully derived* from existing redemption rows. Nothing is stored, no job runs, and the next local day arriving is what restores a repeatable reward — exactly the D10 mechanism by which a completed chore becomes incomplete at midnight.

Both predicates are *scoped per-kid*. ~kid_id~ on a reward is an audience control — who the reward is offered to — and carries no scarcity meaning whatever.

Let ~spends(reward, kid)~ be the redemption rows for that reward /and that kid/ with ~approved_at IS NOT NULL AND reversed_at IS NULL~.

#+begin_src
one-time  (repeatable = false):
  consumed?     = spends(reward, kid) is non-empty     -- this kid, ANY day
  available?    = not consumed?

repeatable (repeatable = true):
  on_cooldown?  = spends(reward, kid) has a row with local_date = today
  available?    = not on_cooldown?
#+end_src

One uniform scope, one mental model: *every availability question is asked about one kid at a time.* "One-time" means /each kid may claim it once, ever/; "repeatable" means /each kid may claim it once per day/ (SC-5). Whether a reward is offered to one kid or to both changes nothing about how either predicate evaluates — ~kid_id~ decides *who sees it*, never *how many exist*.

A reversal *restores availability for free*. Both predicates exclude reversed rows, so a parent reversing a redemption returns a one-time reward to that child's catalog and clears a repeatable reward's cooldown, with no extra code path (SC-8).

Pending requests *do not consume*. A pending row has no ~approved_at~, so it is invisible to both predicates — as is a withdrawn one, which never had one either (D76). Under per-kid scoping there is *no cross-kid race to close*: two kids holding pending requests for the same reward is simply two independent asks, and both may legitimately be approved.

A *same-kid* interleaving does remain possible, and it is why the approval-time re-check validates availability as well as affordability: a child asks for a reward on the kiosk, the parent direct-redeems that same reward for that same child from admin, and then approves the still-open request — which would claim a one-time reward twice, or a repeatable one twice in a day. The re-check catches it and tells the parent which check failed.

Card state is *always evaluated for the kid whose column it is*. With both predicates per-kid this is trivially true rather than an invariant needing maintenance: nothing on a child's shelf can change because of something the sibling did.

The open-request index *does not interact with any of this*. Its key is ~(kid_id, reward_id, local_date)~ (D77), so it forbids a *second simultaneous ask for the same reward on the same day* and nothing else — it never decides whether a reward is claimable, which is availability's job. Repeatability, kid scoping, and the duplicate-ask guard remain three independent constraints; how many rewards a kid may have open at once is decided by neither, but by the reserve-aware rendering rule below.

** Affordability — strict on both legs

One rule, one function, both legs:

#+begin_src
Points.balance(kid, today) >= reward.points
#+end_src

The *true signed sum*, never the floored display figure — D41 pre-authorizes this in as many words. There is no parent override and no IOU concept: a parent's direct-redeem is bound by exactly the same check as a child's request (SC-4).

Checked twice:

1. *At request time* (kiosk) — an unaffordable reward renders visibly locked and is not tappable, so the check is a rendering rule before it is a validation. That rendering rule is the reserve-aware form below, which is stricter than this one, never looser.
2. *At approval time* (admin) — affordability *and* availability are re-validated, because the balance and the catalog both move between ask and answer. This is the domain gate, and it stays the only one.

Pending requests *contribute nothing to the ledger*. A pending row contributes 0 to the balance and has no effect on the total or on the badge, and there is *no domain hold*: escrowing a pending price against the balance itself would create two balances (true and available) that the always-visible badge (D43) cannot show at once. That rejection stands (D63) and is not what the rule below is.

What the kiosk *renders* is reserve-aware (D77). A kid may hold several open requests at once, bounded by a rule that lives entirely in the card-state derivation:

#+begin_src
locked?  =  Points.balance(kid, today) − Rewards.pending_total(kid, today)  <  reward.points
#+end_src

~pending_total/2~ sums this kid's open requests' snapshot prices; ~Points.balance/2~ is untouched by it. So a child can ask for several things their points cover, and the moment the set would exceed what they have, the remaining cards lock with the same glyph they already use for "not enough" — the pre-reader-legible explanation D64 relies on, needing no number. The badge keeps showing the true floored total, never an available-after-commitments figure.

The race this closes — a child piling up mutually exclusive asks the parent must then decline for money the child was never shown — is closed at the point the child acts. The approval-time re-check remains the backstop and is order-safe: approving one ask can make another's re-check fail as unaffordable, which is expected and already surfaces as a named refusal.

** Hidden debt: bounded, and made visible to the party who can act

A child at true balance 2 who then takes a fail sits at true −3 while the badge honestly shows 0. Redemption does not create this — D41 accepted it explicitly ("two kids both showing 0 may have different true standings") — but redemption *widens* it, because a spend is now a routine way to arrive near the floor.

No spend-time guard can close it. Affordability already guarantees a spend never drives the balance below zero; all residual debt comes from *fails after* a spend, which is by construction outside any spend-time check.

The resolution is not to hide less on the kiosk but to hide nothing in admin:

- *Kiosk keeps the floor* (D41, D43). The *lock glyph on an unaffordable card is the pre-reader-legible explanation* — a child who cannot read a negative number can read a lock. No number is needed for the child to act correctly (SC-3, SC-6).
- *Admin displays the true signed balance*, negative and all, wherever it shows a balance (the approval queue's per-kid header band, the direct-redeem picker). The parent is a reader, not a pre-reader, and is the only party who can act on debt.

** Kiosk interaction

One tap to ask — *no modal, no confirmation*, with ~phx-throttle~ matching the shipped chore row. This is the deliberate contrast to D53: D53 guards actions that are *irreversible* and punitive, and a request is neither — the parent gate *is* the protection, and a mistaken ask costs a decline. A confirmation dialog is also the wrong instrument on this surface, since a pre-reader cannot read it.

One tap to take it back, too (D76): tapping a pending card *withdraws* the request, with no confirmation, because re-asking is the undo. The other escapes — the parent's decline and the overnight lapse — remain.

Dismiss and idle auto-return: the view closes three ways (D75) — an explicit ✕, the banner gift button used as a toggle, and an idle timer that returns the column to its normal state if the child wanders off. The timer is a ~Process.send_after~ armed on open, reset on each tap inside the view (a request and a withdraw are both in-view taps and both reset it), and cancelled on dismiss. The duration is a module constant tuned at the on-device gate, exactly as ~@collapse_delay_ms~ (currently 400ms) is; this design deliberately does not pin a number, though ~30s is the starting guess.

Feedback stays *at the point of action* (D75). After a successful request the view does *not* return to the normal column: the tapped card transitions in place to its pending rendering (row 3), which is the acknowledgment, and the shop stays open so the child can keep looking or take the ask back. The banner button flips to =⏳= as well, but as ambient state rather than as the only feedback. Because a request no longer closes the view, the idle timer is the ordinary way a shop left open ends.

State is *ephemeral*: ~:rewards~ membership is a socket-level ~MapSet~ of kid ids, exactly like the shipped ~@expanded~ — never persisted, cleared by the timer, and cleared on the routine boundary re-render.

** Live updates

~BearCub.Rewards~ owns PubSub topic ="rewards"= and broadcasts a payload-free ~:rewards_changed~ on every successful write. ~KioskLive~ and ~Admin.TodayLive~ subscribe to it *in addition to* ="chores"=, and re-fetch — never patching from a payload.

This is the shipped ~Calendars.subscribe/0~ / ~:calendars_changed~ shape exactly: ~KioskLive~ already handles two independent topics with two ~handle_info/2~ clauses, so the pattern costs one clause per subscriber and introduces nothing new.

** The points aggregate rewrite (claimed batch member)

The problem, sized: ~points_total/2~ issues one query for distinct ~(routine, local_date)~ pairs, then calls ~routine_day_contribution/3~ — two more queries — per pair. After a year of use that is roughly 2 routines × 365 days ≈ 1,460 queries per kid, per render, and it runs on every ~:chores_changed~ broadcast on every connected surface. Correctness is unaffected today, which is why it was deferred. Redemption makes it load-bearing three ways: the affordability check runs on *every tap* and on every reward-card render, the badge reads it, and admin reads it for every balance display. It lands *early in the batch*, before affordability depends on it (SC-9).

The target shape: ~Points.balances(local_date) :: %{kid_id => signed_integer}~ — O(1) queries for an entire render, not O(kids × days). Three grouped queries:

1. *Extras* — one aggregate over ~completions~ joined to ~chores~ where ~routine IS NULL~, summing a ~CASE~ over ~failed_at~ / ~undone_at~ (the shipped ~extra_contribution/2~ ladder expressed in SQL), grouped by ~kid_id~.
2. *Routine-days* — one query grouping completions by ~(kid_id, routine, local_date)~ computing a live-completion count and an any-failed boolean, joined against a per-~(kid_id, routine)~ chore-count subquery; ~complete? = chore_count > 0 AND live_count == chore_count~, ~failed?~ from the boolean, then the capped ~+R~ / ~−R~ arithmetic.
3. *Redemptions* — one aggregate summing ~points~ over rows with ~approved_at IS NOT NULL AND reversed_at IS NULL~, grouped by ~kid_id~.

~Points.balance(kid, local_date)~ delegates to the map for a single kid, so tests and single-kid admin reads keep an ergonomic entry point. ~Points.total~ / ~Points.totals~ apply the D41 floor on top.

There is *no memoization, no cached totals, no materialized column* — the derived-state doctrine (D10) is not negotiable here, and the aggregate is fast enough that caching would be complexity buying nothing.

Discovered property — *roster drift*, surfaced but not fixed: ~routine_day_contribution/3~ loads the kid's *current* chore roster for the routine and requires every one of those chores to have a live completion on the evaluated date. Historical routine-days are therefore evaluated against today's roster: adding a morning chore today retroactively makes every past morning-day incomplete, wiping all historical ~+R~; deleting one can retroactively complete past days and grant points that were never earned. This ships today, and the badge already moves when a parent edits chores. Redemption raises the stakes: a roster edit can move a balance *after* a spend, potentially into debt with no fail involved.

Semantics stay *unchanged* in the rewrite — it is a pure performance refactor, proven behavior-preserving by tests — and roster drift is captured as its own backlog entry at initiative close. The claimed item is the query shape, not the derivation's semantics.

The pinning tests *must include a roster-drift case* — a routine-day evaluated after the roster changed — so behavior preservation is proven on the weird path and not merely the happy one. A refactor that silently "fixed" drift would be an unruled semantic change smuggled in under a performance story.

** Testing and the on-device gate

Per repo convention, LiveView integration tests are part of story ACs wherever a flow is touched, regardless of the mvp DoD.

- *Unit* — redemption contribution across all marker combinations; the pending / lapsed / approved / reversed / declined / withdrawn derivations, including withdraw's own state guard in both directions (withdraw refused on an answered row, approve/decline refused on a withdrawn one); ~pending_total/2~ and the reserve-aware lock condition, including several open asks at once and the re-keyed duplicate-ask index; one-time consumption and repeatable cooldown, both at per-kid scope — including a sibling's redemption of the same reward leaving this kid's card untouched, and the reversal-restores-availability path; affordability against a negative true balance; and the ~Points.balances~ aggregate *pinned against the current per-kid implementation*, including the roster-drift case.

- *LiveView integration* — kiosk: open, dismiss (✕ and the banner toggle), idle auto-return, request-stays-in-shop with the card turning pending in place, withdraw returning the card to its plain state, and each of the seven card states; admin: approve, decline, direct-redeem, reverse, archive-hides-from-catalog-but-not-history, and the approval-time re-check failing on both affordability and availability; cross-surface PubSub: a kiosk request appearing in the admin queue, and an approval moving the kiosk badge.

- *Time* — every test pins ~:routine_windows~ per ~docs/learnings.org~ [2026-07-17] rather than inheriting the wall clock, and all new derivations take a local-date argument. Day-rollover behavior (lapse, cooldown reset, verdict-state expiry) is tested by *passing a different local date*, never by mocking a clock.

- *On-device gate* (per repo convention a manual human checklist at gate close — never CI'd, never skipped) — the reward view rendering in Fully Kiosk's Android WebView; the gift button's tap target at fridge distance and at a child's height; the dim+glyph states distinguishable at a glance across the kitchen; the idle-timer duration *tuned there*, not at a desk; and explicit confirmation that ~:rows~ still meets the 5-chore no-scroll budget with the banner button present.

* Out of Scope for This Design

Per the PRD's Out of Scope, restated as design boundaries:

- *Changing how points are earned* — the earnings derivation is read, renamed (~Chores.earnings/2~), and re-homed under ~Points~, never re-derived. Streaks are an earnings-side mechanic and are out.
- *Fulfillment tracking* — this design lets a parent see that a redemption *happened*; it is not a fulfillment tracker. No ~fulfilled_at~, no "purchased" checkbox. At two-kid scale the verbal channel covers it, and deferral is structurally free: a nullable-timestamp marker plus one admin control later is a trivially additive oneshot. Swept to the backlog at initiative close as an untriaged entry — /"fulfillment marker on redemptions; act if double-buy or forgot-to-buy occurs in family use"/.
- *Family-scarce rewards* — a reward that is a single physical object, claimable once across the *whole family* rather than once per kid, is a real and interesting concept ("there is one trampoline, whoever saves for it first gets it") — but it is deliberately not what ~repeatable = false~ means here. Modeling it would need a scarcity notion orthogonal to both existing knobs, plus a first-come contention story on the kiosk that a pre-reader could read. Not designed for here, and not to be smuggled in by reading scarcity into ~kid_id~.
- *A points-stats view for the kids, and the skip-a-chore admin action* — not claimed, and not designed for. Neither is precluded: the points badge's own tap gesture stays unbound, and nothing in the redemption derivation constrains what a "skipped" chore could contribute, since redemptions sum into the signed-inputs term independently of how earnings are derived.
- *Notifications of any kind* when a request is made or answered. The parent-notification backlog entry is separately ruled and unclaimed.
- *Roster drift* — surfaced above; semantics unchanged in the rewrite, captured as its own backlog entry at initiative close.
- *Not built:* reward categories or tags; per-kid pricing of a shared reward; scheduled or expiring rewards; a wishlist or savings-goal concept; pagination or filtering in history; hard deletion of rewards. (Kid-side withdrawal *is* built — see Key Behaviors → kiosk interaction, D76.)

** [2026-07-25 Sat 20:41] §Data Model, §Key Behaviors — one open request per kid per day is overturned, and the index is re-keyed (D77)

/Sections affected: §Data Model — indexes; §Key Behaviors — availability, affordability; §Kiosk — card states (row 6); §Kiosk — the banner button/

This document previously capped a child at *one* open ask per local day, enforced by ~CREATE UNIQUE INDEX redemptions_one_pending_per_kid ON redemptions (kid_id, local_date) WHERE requested_at IS NOT NULL AND approved_at IS NULL AND declined_at IS NULL~ — an index whose key deliberately had *no reward component*, described here as constraining "how many asks a kid has open, never which reward they may ask for." Three superseded claims rode on it and are the trap: that the two-asks-they-can-only-afford-one-of race is "closed by the approval-time re-check plus the one-open-request-per-kid index, at zero display cost"; that the kiosk lock condition is the bare ~Points.balance(kid, today) < reward.points~; and that "a verdict returns [the banner button] to =🎁=", which held only while at most one ask could be open. The index is now keyed ~(kid_id, reward_id, local_date)~ with a fourth NULL condition on ~withdrawn_at~, and it is a *replacement* — a drop-and-recreate migration, since SQLite cannot alter an index in place; do not write a migration that merely adds one. What did *not* change, and should not be re-argued: a pending row still contributes 0 and no domain hold escrows anything against the balance — that rejection stands (D63); the new bound is a rendering rule in the card-state derivation only.
